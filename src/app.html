About the numerics

Incompressible Navier–Stokes in a box periodic in x and z, with a no-slip wall at y = 0 and a free-slip, rigid-lid surface at y = h (∂u/∂y = ∂w/∂y = 0, v = 0). Lengths are scaled by h and velocities by the bulk velocity Ub, so ν = 1/Reb.

Second-order central differences on a staggered (MAC) grid, with the convective terms in divergence form. The grid is uniform in x and z and clustered at the wall with a tanh mapping in y; the surface end is left coarse.

Time stepping uses the low-storage three-stage Runge–Kutta scheme of Wray for convection and the x–z viscous terms, and Crank–Nicolson for the wall-normal viscous term. Each stage ends with a projection: the pressure Poisson equation is solved exactly with FFTs in x and z (modified wavenumbers) and a tridiagonal solve in y. After every stage a uniform pressure gradient restores Ub = 1, so the mass flux stays constant. Δt follows the CFL number you set.

The initial field is a mean profile plus a few divergence-free large-scale modes (streamwise and spanwise wavelengths of the box size and its halves and thirds) with a little noise. Reτ is taken from the mean wall shear. The default box (π × π/2 at Reb = 2000, Reτ ≈ 130) is just above the minimal flow unit, and Δx⁺ ≈ 13 and Δz⁺ ≈ 6.5 are coarse for a second-order scheme, so treat the statistics as indicative.
